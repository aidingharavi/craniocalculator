    Cephalic Index

    Maximum head width as a percentage of maximum length. The result updates as you type.

    mm
    mm

    Method

    Cephalic index = width ÷ length × 100

    Normal values vary with age, sex, and population, and published cutoffs differ. The bands here follow those commonly used in the positional brachycephaly literature. Age- and sex-specific normative percentiles for the first year of life have been published and are preferable for borderline values.

    A low index suggests a long, narrow head, as seen in sagittal synostosis (scaphocephaly) and in positional dolichocephaly of preterm infants. A high index suggests brachycephaly, which may be positional or reflect bicoronal synostosis. The index alone cannot distinguish these causes.

    Severity bands as used in J Clin Med. 2020;9(4):1027 (PMC7231243). Normative data: Wilbrand JF, et al. Clinical classification of infant nonsynostotic cranial deformity. J Pediatr. 2012;161(6). Find on PubMed.
